Archiwar

Archiwar is the real-time strategy game I've been building in Godot, and I'm aiming to put it on Steam. It's set on Tidemark, a planet that's almost all ocean. The water keeps rising, and the few islands left are what everyone is fighting over.

How it plays

You win by wiping the other side off the map. Knock out their construction yard and they can't build anything new, but whatever they have left keeps fighting until every building is gone. Every building also claims the ground around it, and the land you hold pays rent.

There are two kinds of money. Alloy comes out of mines and pays for machines, and it only counts once a harvester has hauled it back to your yard. Cash pays for people. It comes from the land you hold and arrives as a payday every two minutes. Neutral villages sit on the islands in between, and if you keep troops next to one long enough it switches to your side and starts paying you too.

Vehicles need someone inside to drive them. If a tank's crew gets killed, the tank just sits there until somebody climbs in, and that can be the other side. Sending tanks forward without infantry nearby is a good way to lose them.

You can bridge a strait to walk your army across, but the bridge also blocks your own ships from using that channel.

Damage shows. A building catches fire in more places as it gets weaker, and rubble piles up around it. Hurt vehicles trail smoke, and a timber bridge loses planks until you can see the water through it.

The islands

Some maps have a volcano on one of the middle islands. Its lava runs down the slopes in channels that shift every couple of minutes. Anything standing in it burns, palms next to it catch fire, and nothing can be built on the cone.

The larger islands rise to cliffs along parts of the coast, and a few have a river that runs across the high ground and drops into the sea.

There are things in the water too. A sea serpent lives in open water and bites any ship that sits still near it for too long. The Drift can call up a giant whale that throws itself onto a stretch of coast and flattens whatever is there.

Some maps also have ruins from an older world standing in the shallows. Keep troops at one with nobody from the other side around and you take it, along with a bonus for your whole army.

Factions

There are four factions. The Meridian Charter is a corporate army and plays the most like a standard RTS side. The Kindled fight with fire at close range, and their commander talks like a pro wrestler. The Drift live on rafts and are strongest at sea. The Levee Syndicate pump out the sea to make new land, then dig in on it.

Each faction unlocks its stronger units its own way. The Kindled build up fame, the Drift build up devotion through their priests, the Syndicate count the land they have made, and the Charter pays for it. The campaign starts with the Charter.

The Drift can also build on the water. Their guns, lookouts and workshops can go up on rafts, but only on water they already hold close to shore, and a raft doesn't take as much punishment as solid ground.

A lot of the machinery moves. Guns turn to follow their targets, windmills turn in the wind, and the Syndicate's pile driver hammers away.

Music

Each faction has five tracks that share one tune: a main theme, a battle track that comes in when your side is under fire, and three quieter ones for the rest of the match. They're synthesised in code rather than recorded, with the 1995 Command & Conquer soundtrack as the inspiration. Here is each faction's main theme.
