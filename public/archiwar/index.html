Archiwar

A real-time strategy game I'm building in Godot for Steam. Four factions fight over the last islands of a drowning planet.

How it plays In battle Factions The islands The campaign

It's set on Tidemark, a planet that's almost all ocean. The water keeps rising, and the few islands left are what everyone is fighting over.

How it plays

You win by wiping the other side off the map. Knock out their construction yard and they can't build anything new, but their barracks and factories keep working for as long as there is alloy to pay for it. Every building also claims the ground around it, and in a skirmish, holding 45% of the dry land for two and a half minutes wins it outright.

There are two kinds of money. Alloy pays for machines and buildings. Harvesters dig it out of the mines, and it only counts once they've hauled it back to your base. Cash pays for people. It comes from the land you hold and arrives as a payday every two minutes. Neutral villages sit on the islands in between, and if you keep troops next to one long enough, it joins you and pays taxes too.

Buildings work the way they did in Command & Conquer. You pay for one in the sidebar, it gets built there, and when it's ready you place it on the map. Infantry, vehicles, buildings and research each have their own queue, so nothing holds up anything else.

Machines need someone inside to drive them. A tank comes out of the factory empty and waits for a soldier to climb in. If its crew gets killed, it sits there until somebody else does, and that can be the other side. Planes take off with their pilot aboard, and if the pilot is killed, the plane falls out of the sky.

You can bridge a strait to walk your army across, but the bridge also blocks your own ships from using that channel. Every boat comes off a naval yard, so an island war needs a coast to build on.

Palm groves are where wood comes from, and wood builds bridges. Soldiers can cut it, and timber rigs cut it faster. Groves also burn.

In battle

Fights happen at range, and every side fights its own way. The Syndicate's machines carry shields and their drones burn targets from above. At sea, shells that miss throw up water and a hit hull flashes white.

Damage shows. A building catches fire in more places as it gets weaker, and rubble piles up around it. Hurt vehicles trail smoke, and a timber bridge loses planks until you can see the water through it.

Soldiers fall the way they were killed. A man who is shot buckles at the knees and goes over away from the gun, a shell throws him off his feet, and fire turns him round before he drops. The bodies stay a moment, then the ground takes them.

Factions

There are four factions, and each unlocks its stronger units its own way. The Kindled build up fame, the Drift build up devotion through their priests, the Syndicate count the land they have made, and the Charter's Assembly simply pays for it.

The Meridian Charter

The planet's own coalition, trying to unify the islands. It plays the most like a standard RTS side, and everything in its base is numbered and in order.

The Kindled

Rebels who want to burn the old order down and have no idea what comes after. They fight with fire at close range, from a Charter office they took and burned out.

The Drift

Religious fanatics who live on rafts and pray to the sea. Their anchorage is a ship hauled up the beach, waiting for the water to rise and float it off.

The Levee Syndicate

A corporation back from Earth for the land and what is under it. They pump out the sea to make new ground, then build on it in white composite and Earth money.

The Drift can also build on the water. Their guns, lookouts and workshops can go up on rafts, but only on water they already hold close to shore, and a raft doesn't take as much punishment as solid ground.

Soldiers are trained in a barracks, and each side builds its own kind.

The Syndicate's army is as expensive as their buildings. Contractors in white plate carry rifles, Exosuits fire a laser lance from a powered frame, Compliance Officers hold a riot shield made of light, and Service Drones fly in to repair whatever is damaged. The Arbiter is a slow laser tank, the Mass Driver a railgun that shells from range, and the Hover Lander puts eight troops on a beach. Their strike comes from orbit. A pod lands on the target, and a squad walks out of it.

Every faction also fields two machines of its own, each based on a real vehicle. The Charter have the Beachhead, a hovercraft gunboat that runs over sea and sand alike, and the Long Arm, a mortar on a hydraulic boom. The Kindled have the Drumroll, an armoured dozer that rolls a burning drum into buildings, and the Trapdoor, a tunnelling machine you cannot see until it comes up to fight. The Drift have the Pilgrim, a walking excavator in a salvaged hull that wades out of the surf, and the Undertow, a submarine. The Syndicate have the Gangway, which lays a bridge across a channel, and the Kulik, a fast amphibious scout.

A lot of the machinery moves. Guns turn to follow their targets, fires burn, and rotors and fans spin.

The islands

Some maps have a volcano on one of the middle islands. Lava runs down its slopes in channels that shift every couple of minutes. Anything standing in it burns, palms next to it catch fire, and nothing can be built on the cone.

The larger islands rise to cliffs along parts of the coast, and a few have a river that starts at a spring, runs across the high ground and drops into the sea.

There are things in the water too. A sea serpent lives in open water and bites any ship that sits still near it for too long. The Drift can call up a giant whale that throws itself onto a stretch of coast and flattens whatever is there. Some maps also have ruins from an older world standing in the shallows. Keep troops at one with nobody from the other side around and you take it, along with a bonus for your whole army.

The campaign

The campaign is played as the Meridian Charter, the islands' own coalition, trying to bring all of Tidemark under one charter. Corporal Marsh talks you through it, and he turns out to know more about the one surveyor who never signed than he first lets on. Later chapters bring in the other three factions, and beating the campaign unlocks them for skirmish.

The first mission is training. Each step lights up what it wants you to click, and it ends with your first real fight. A new campaign asks how hard you want the other side to play.
