Archiwar

Archiwar is the real-time strategy game I've been building in Godot, and I'm aiming to put it on Steam. It's set on Tidemark, a planet that's almost all ocean. The water keeps rising, and the few islands left are what everyone is fighting over.

How it plays

You win by wiping the other side off the map. Knock out their construction yard and they can't build anything new, but whatever they have left keeps fighting until every building is gone. Every building also claims the ground around it, and in a skirmish, holding 45% of the dry land for two and a half minutes wins it outright.

There are two kinds of money. Alloy pays for machines and buildings. Harvesters dig it out of the mines, and it only counts once they've hauled it back to your yard. Cash pays for people. It comes from the land you hold and arrives as a payday every two minutes. Neutral villages sit on the islands in between, and if you keep troops next to one long enough, it joins you and pays taxes too.

Buildings work the way they did in Command & Conquer. You pay for one in the sidebar, it gets built there, and when it's ready you place it on the map. Infantry, vehicles, buildings and research each have their own queue, so nothing holds up anything else.

Machines need someone inside to drive them. A tank comes out of the factory empty and waits for a soldier to climb in. If its crew gets killed, it sits there until somebody else does, and that can be the other side. Planes take off with their pilot aboard, and if the pilot is killed, the plane falls out of the sky.

You can bridge a strait to walk your army across, but the bridge also blocks your own ships from using that channel. Every boat comes off a naval yard, so an island war needs a coast to build on.

Damage shows. A building catches fire in more places as it gets weaker, and rubble piles up around it. Hurt vehicles trail smoke, and a timber bridge loses planks until you can see the water through it.

Palm groves are where wood comes from, and wood builds bridges. Soldiers can cut it, and timber rigs cut it faster. Groves also burn.

The campaign

The campaign is played as the Meridian Charter, a company army that has just arrived on Tidemark to map the islands and dig for ore. Corporal Marsh talks you through it, and he turns out to know more about the people already living out here than he first lets on. Later chapters bring in the other three factions. Beating the campaign unlocks the other three for skirmish.

The first mission is training. Each step lights up what it wants you to click, and it ends with your first real fight. A new campaign asks how hard you want the other side to play.

The islands

Some maps have a volcano on one of the middle islands. Lava runs down its slopes in channels that shift every couple of minutes. Anything standing in it burns, palms next to it catch fire, and nothing can be built on the cone.

The larger islands rise to cliffs along parts of the coast, and a few have a river that starts at a spring, runs across the high ground and drops into the sea.

There are things in the water too. A sea serpent lives in open water and bites any ship that sits still near it for too long. The Drift can call up a giant whale that throws itself onto a stretch of coast and flattens whatever is there.

Some maps also have ruins from an older world standing in the shallows. Keep troops at one with nobody from the other side around and you take it, along with a bonus for your whole army.

Factions

There are four factions. The Meridian Charter is a company army and plays the most like a standard RTS side. The Kindled want to burn the old order down and start again, and have no idea what comes after. They fight with fire at close range and make a show of it. The Drift live on rafts, pray to the sea and are strongest on it. The Levee Syndicate pump out the sea to make new land, then dig in on it.

Each faction unlocks its stronger units its own way. The Kindled build up fame, the Drift build up devotion through their priests, the Syndicate count the land they have made, and the Charter simply pays for it.

The Drift can also build on the water. Their guns, lookouts and workshops can go up on rafts, but only on water they already hold close to shore, and a raft doesn't take as much punishment as solid ground.

A lot of the machinery moves. Guns turn to follow their targets, windmills turn in the wind, and the Syndicate's pile driver hammers away.
